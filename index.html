Hogger, Warlord of the Riverpaw

A reimagined 5-player encounter, written for World of Warcraft: Forever's expanded early-game content.

Format
5-Player Instanced
Setting
Elwynn Forest
Content Type
Outdoor-World Boss
Phases
Three, Health-Gated

The Riverpaw Don't Forget

A short quest chain sets up the encounter before it begins.

Marshal McBride — Northshire Abbey

"Used to be, Hogger was a joke. Something you laughed about after you outleveled him. But the caravans coming up from Goldshire this week, they're saying the Riverpaw's not scattering like they used to. They're regrouping. Digging in around the old camp east of the crossroads. Somebody's holding that pack together, and it isn't fear of us anymore."

  1. Investigate the abandoned caravan wreckage near the Fold. Gnoll totems are arranged with unusual deliberateness, not scavenged, placed.
  2. Interrogate a captured Riverpaw scout who won't break under threat, but breaks a little at the mention of a name, said with something closer to reverence than the old snarling.
  3. Approach the camp itself at dusk. Multiple fires burn in a ring formation. This is the encounter's cold open, the staging ground before the fight starts.

Concept

Why reimagine a starter-zone joke as a real fight

Hogger was never actually dangerous, he was just disproportionate for a level 6 character. The joke was the gap between his reputation and his mechanics. This encounter closes that gap on purpose. Same Hogger, same Riverpaw gnolls, same Elwynn camp, but built as if the community's decade of memes about him were true all along. The goal is recognition, not surprise: players should feel like they're finally getting the fight they always joked he should have been.

The Encounter

Existing Riverpaw camp, expanded. Tents ring a central bonfire, smaller campfires hold the perimeter.

Phase 1 — The Warband
Tent Spawns Add Pressure

Hogger doesn't start alone. Two to three Riverpaw gnoll adds are already up, pulled from the surrounding tents. An unaddressed tent periodically pushes a fresh add into the fight.

Design intent:The environment is something you fight, not just stand in. Tents turn the camp itself into a pressure source from the first pull.
Collapse Tent Player Action

Players can interact with a tent and channel briefly to shut off that spawn point permanently, trading time and exposure for reduced add pressure later.

Design intent:A visible, optional cost-benefit choice in Phase 1, not a required mechanic, but one with a real payoff two phases later.
Phase 2 — Riverpaw Don't Break
Camp-Wide Alarm Phase Transition

At 66%, Hogger drags a Riverpaw totem into the central bonfire. Every remaining tent pushes an add simultaneously, once.

Design intent:Direct payoff for Phase 1's tent-collapsing, not a flat difficulty gate. A group that prepared faces a proportionally smaller wave.
Bonfire Zeal Buff

Hogger gains a temporary damage buff tied to proximity to the central bonfire he's trying to hold.

Design intent:Incentivizes fighting the encounter away from the fire, turning positioning into an active decision instead of a static tank spot.
Phase 3 — The Old Fear
The Fire Goes Out Phase Transition

At 33%, Hogger extinguishes the central bonfire himself, a scripted animation beat. In the dark, his damage output increases.

Design intent:The camp's centerpiece flips from Hogger's power source to the source of the fight's final threat.
Campfire Sanctuary Signature Mechanic

Every perimeter campfire still standing casts a small radius of light that suppresses Hogger's darkness buff while players stand in it.

Design intent:The fight's signature moment. The same environmental objects that were a spawn hazard in Phase 1 become the only source of safety in Phase 3, driven entirely by the story beat, not a new asset.

Pacing and Tuning Intent

Phase transitions are health-gated, not timer-gated, rewarding damage output over clock management. There is no hard enrage. Failure pressure instead comes from unaddressed tent spawns compounding into Phase 2's alarm wave, a group that brute-forces Phase 1 creates its own difficulty spike rather than facing an arbitrary timer. Exact phase durations and specific health thresholds would be set through playtesting against real group DPS, not fixed at design time.

Why This Works as a Pitch Example

Every mechanic maps to something that already exists in the zone, tents, campfires, gnoll pack behavior, rather than introducing new lore or assets. Reimagining existing content credibly is a different, and arguably harder, design skill than inventing from nothing, and it's the skill Forever's expanded-zone content actually needs.
